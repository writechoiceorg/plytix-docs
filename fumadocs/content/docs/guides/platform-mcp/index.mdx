The Plytix MCP connects a compatible AI application to your Plytix account, so you can ask your assistant to work with your product data in natural language. This guide covers what the Plytix MCP is, connecting your account and checking the connection, the capabilities in this release, a few example workflows to start from, and what to do if something doesn't work.

The Plytix MCP signs in with OAuth, so you authorize your AI application in the browser instead of pasting a token. What your assistant can do depends on two factors: the tools in the current release and the access you set for each tool in your AI application. Some tools can change your catalog, so try the example workflows on a test account first.

## What is the Plytix MCP?

The Plytix MCP is a server that lets an AI application, such as an AI assistant or agent, work with the data in your Plytix account. It uses the Model Context Protocol (MCP), an open standard that lets AI applications discover and call tools exposed by external services.

Once you connect it, your assistant finds the Plytix tools on its own and calls them when your request needs your account's data. You ask in plain language, and you don't call tools by name.

The Plytix MCP isn't an API version, and it doesn't give your assistant every endpoint in the [V3 API](/reference/v3). It offers the tools listed in [Available capabilities](#available-capabilities), and that list grows from release to release.

It's also separate from the [Documentation MCP](/docs/guides/documentation-mcp), which reads these docs, needs no account, and can't see your data. Connecting one doesn't connect the other.

## Connect your account

To connect, you add the Plytix MCP to your AI application as a remote MCP server, then sign in to Plytix with OAuth to authorize it.

### Prerequisites

Before you start, make sure you have the following:

- Plytix MCP access turned on for your Plytix account. To request it, contact your account manager.
- An AI application that supports remote MCP servers and OAuth sign-in. Your application or workspace may also need an administrator to approve custom connections.

You'll need the following connection details:

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Server URL | `https://pim.plytix.com/mcp` |
| Authentication | OAuth |

The Plytix MCP is separate from the V3 API, so you don't need V3 or a [V3 access token](/reference/v3/authentication) to connect. The OAuth sign-in authorizes your AI application, separately from the access tokens in the **API v3** tab.

### Add the Plytix MCP to your AI application

Follow the steps below to add the server, authorize it, and enable it.

1. **Open** the custom connector or MCP server settings in your AI application.
2. **Add** a connection named `Plytix` and enter the server URL `https://pim.plytix.com/mcp`.
3. **Choose** OAuth as the authentication method, then follow the prompts to sign in to Plytix.
4. **Complete** the authorization when Plytix asks you to.
5. **Set** the access for each Plytix tool in your application's connector settings, if your application offers this. For example, you can let a tool run on its own, require your approval each time it runs, or block it.
6. **Return** to your AI application and enable the Plytix connection for your conversation, if your application asks you to.

To disconnect, remove the Plytix connection in your AI application's settings.

For account and workspace requirements in a specific application, see [Claude's guide to custom connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-get-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp) or [OpenAI's guide to custom MCP servers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/custom-mcp-server).

### Verify the connection

Follow the steps below to confirm that your AI application found the Plytix tools and can read your account.

1. **Check** that your application lists the Plytix tools. Most applications show a connection's tools in its settings, or next to the connector in a conversation.
2. **Ask** what the connection can do:

   ```text
   What can you do with my Plytix account through this connection?
   ```

   A working connection answers with the capabilities in this release. This confirms that the tools were found, but not that your assistant can read your data.

3. **Run** a small read that you can check yourself, such as the first workflow in [Example workflows](#find-a-product):

   ```text
   Using Plytix, find the product with SKU GF-Basket and show its name and description.
   ```

   Compare the answer against the product in Plytix. If the values match, the connection works and your account is reachable. If the answer has no specifics, your assistant probably answered without calling Plytix. See [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting).

## Available capabilities

Your AI application discovers the Plytix tools directly from the server. Each tool comes with a description and an input schema, which tell the application what the tool does and what it needs. The live tool definitions are always current, so to see the exact tools available to you, ask your assistant or check the connection in your application's settings. Your application lists each tool by a short title, such as "Get product list".

Each capability below is made up of several tools, such as one that searches and one that updates. Because you set access for each tool, you can let read tools run on their own and require your approval for tools that change your catalog.

## Example workflows

These prompts describe tasks you might hand to your assistant. Each one names what it needs from the release, so skip any that the [current capabilities](#available-capabilities) don't cover. Start your prompt with "Using Plytix," so your assistant knows to call the Plytix tools, then adapt the SKUs and fields to your own catalog.

### Find a product

Look up one product and read back its values.

```text
Using Plytix, find the product with SKU GF-Basket and show its name and description.
```

Your assistant gets the matching product and reports the values Plytix returned. This workflow needs product lookup and access to those fields.

### Understand your catalog structure

Ask your assistant to explain how your attributes are set up.

```text
Using Plytix, list the product attributes available in my account and explain which ones describe product dimensions.
```

Your assistant gets your attribute definitions and explains the relevant ones, such as `product_length_mm` or `product_weight_kg`. This workflow needs attribute discovery.

### Review content quality

Check a small set of products for missing content, without changing anything.

```text
Using Plytix, inspect 10 products in the Drinkware category and identify which have an empty description. Show the SKUs and don't change anything.
```

Your assistant reviews the products it gets and lists the SKUs with an empty description. The result covers only those 10 products, so it's a spot check, not an audit of your whole catalog. This workflow needs product search with the relevant fields.

### Draft content for a sales channel

Turn your product data into listing copy, without changing anything in Plytix.

```text
Using Plytix, get the name and description of SKU GF-Basket, and draft a product title and a 150-word description for an Amazon listing. Don't change anything in Plytix.
```

Your assistant reads the product's values and writes the draft in the conversation. Plytix doesn't change. This workflow needs product lookup and access to those fields.

### Check a supplier file before you import it

Compare a supplier's file with your catalog before you import it into Plytix.

```text
Using Plytix, compare the attached supplier file with my catalog. List the SKUs in the file that aren't in Plytix, and the SKUs whose name differs from Plytix. Don't change anything.
```

Your assistant reads the file in your AI application, looks up its SKUs in Plytix, and lists the differences. For a long file, ask how many SKUs it checked and compare that with the file. This workflow needs an AI application that accepts file attachments, and product lookup.

### Answer product questions for your team

Let colleagues in sales or support get product facts from Plytix in chat.

```text
Using Plytix, which category is SKU GF-Basket in, and what does its description say? Show the values you used.
```

Your assistant answers from the values Plytix returns and shows them, so your colleague can check the answer. This workflow needs product lookup and access to the product's categories.

### Improve a product description

Get a suggested rewrite and approve it before anything changes.

```text
Using Plytix, for SKU GF-Basket, propose a clearer description and show me the proposed text before saving anything.
```

Your assistant reads the current description and shows you a suggested rewrite. Nothing changes in Plytix until you approve the text in a second prompt, such as "Save that description to GF-Basket." Saving needs a tool with write access to product attributes.

<Callout type="warn" title="Review before you save">
  A write tool changes your catalog as soon as it runs. Ask for a proposal first, review it, and then approve the change in a separate prompt. Plytix can't undo some changes, so try write workflows on a test account first.
</Callout>

## Limitations and troubleshooting

The Plytix MCP is in beta, so its capabilities are limited to the tools in the current release, and an action that isn't in [Available capabilities](#available-capabilities) isn't available yet. Your assistant also reads product data from suppliers, imports, and integrations. Treat that text as untrusted, and keep reading and writing in separate prompts.

Check the problems below if you run into issues while connecting or using the Plytix MCP.

<Accordions type="multiple">
  <Accordion title="The connection fails" value="connection-fails">
    Check that the server URL matches `https://pim.plytix.com/mcp` exactly, then complete the OAuth authorization again. If your application asks for an administrator's approval for custom connections, the connection can't finish until that approval is given.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="No Plytix tools appear" value="no-tools">
    Check that the Plytix connection is enabled for your conversation, since some applications add a connector without turning it on. If your application has an option to refresh a connection's tool list, use it after a new release so your assistant sees the current tools.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Your assistant says it can't do what you asked" value="action-unavailable">
    Check the request against [Available capabilities](#available-capabilities) first, since the beta covers only the tools in the current release. If the capability is listed, check the access you set for that tool in your AI application's connector settings. A blocked tool can't run, and a tool that needs approval runs only after you approve it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Your assistant answers about your catalog without calling Plytix" value="no-tool-call">
    An answer that sounds plausible but doesn't match your account usually means your assistant answered from its own knowledge instead of calling a tool. Start the prompt with "Using Plytix," and ask your assistant to show the SKUs it retrieved, so you can check the answer against Plytix.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Results are shorter than what Plytix shows" value="short-results">
    Your assistant may have stopped at the first page of results and reported it as the full answer. Ask it to get all the results, and include the expected count in your prompt when you know it, so your assistant can check its answer against that count.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
